Reviewer 1

This paper proposes FC‑GNN, a novel architecture that integrates fuzzy message‑passing with Mondrian conformal prediction for cybersecurity anomaly detection. The idea of combining fuzzy logic and conformal guarantees is timely and relevant. However, the manuscript contains several critical flaws that must be addressed.

1. Table 3 reports that FL‑GNN and FGAT achieve perfect Macro‑F1 (1.000) on five datasets (UNSW‑NB15, NF‑BoT‑IoT, ISCX‑Botnet, CTU‑13, N‑BaIoT, ToN‑IoT). Such flawless performance on real‑world cybersecurity benchmarks is highly implausible and suggests data leakage, improper hyperparameter tuning, or a reporting error. The authors must verify these results, clarify the experimental setup (e.g., whether these baselines were trained on the same chronological split), and correct them if found to be erroneous. Without this, the claimed superiority of FC‑GNN is not credible.

2. The table is incomplete: coverage and APSS values for DAPS, SNAPS, and RR‑GNN on several datasets (e.g., UNSW‑NB15, NF‑BoT‑IoT) are omitted or misaligned. The “Gap” column is also poorly defined. The authors should provide a complete, self‑contained table with all seven datasets and five baselines, clearly reporting empirical coverage, APSS, and the coverage gap formula.

3. The paper acknowledges that concept drift violates exchangeability (Sec. 6.3) but offers only a chronological split as mitigation. No quantitative analysis of drift severity or its impact on coverage degradation is provided. The authors should add experiments with sliding‑window recalibration or non‑exchangeable CP baselines to demonstrate the method’s robustness under drift, or at least characterize when coverage fails.

4. Some minor issues: Figure ?? in Page 8.

Reviewer 2

This paper proposes a fuzzy-conformal GNN FC-GNN for cybersecurity anomaly detection. The method combines fuzzy message passing with Mondrian conformal prediction to produce uncertainty-aware prediction sets with coverage guarantees. The paper evaluates the method on seven cybersecurity datasets, including intrusion detection, botnet, and IoT malware benchmarks.

The paper studies an important problem: reliable and interpretable GNN-based anomaly detection for cybersecurity. However, I still have the following concerns:

- The empirical advantage is not fully convincing. FC-GNN does not consistently outperform strong baselines in point prediction; in Table 3, fuzzy baselines achieve much higher average Macro-F1, and FC-GNN performs poorly on CIC-IDS-2017.
- The coverage claims are somewhat overstated. FC-GNN still falls below the 0.90 target on several datasets, so the paper should more carefully distinguish theoretical guarantees from empirical violations under drift.
- The theoretical guarantee relies on strong assumptions, especially fuzzy permutation invariance and consistent community assignment. These assumptions may not hold in realistic cybersecurity graphs with temporal drift, adversarial behavior, and evolving attack communities.
- The graph construction protocol is not sufficiently clear. Since many tabular cybersecurity datasets are converted into graphs, the paper should better justify node/edge definitions and analyze how sensitive results are to graph construction choices.
- The runtime and scalability discussion is limited. Louvain partitioning, fuzzy rule evaluation, and conformal calibration may become costly on large dynamic enterprise networks.
- The related work misses important GNN security literature. Since the paper targets cybersecurity and robust GNN deployment, it should discuss graph adversarial threats such as node injection attacks and defenses, e.g., GZOO, and DShield.

Reviewer 3

The reviewed paper proposes FC-GNN (Fuzzy-Conformal Graph Neural Network), a framework that attempts to address two major unresolved problems in graph-based cybersecurity anomaly detection: uncertainty caused by imprecise network telemetry and the lack of statistically guaranteed prediction reliability. The authors combine fuzzy graph learning with conformal prediction to construct an uncertainty-aware and theoretically guaranteed anomaly detection framework.

Although FC-GNN provides an interesting combination of fuzzy reasoning and conformal uncertainty estimation, several limitations remain.

1: Fixed global Sugeno λ parameter
The proposed fuzzy nonconformity score relies on a shared λ parameter. However, cybersecurity attack categories often exhibit different structural characteristics: DDoS attacks involve high-volume communication patterns; Botnets exhibit propagation structures; Malware infections may show sparse behavioral chains. A single λ value may not optimally represent all attack communities.

2. Interpretability remains partially empirical rather than theoretically grounded
The fuzzy rules generated by FC-GNN improve interpretability, but the connection between learned fuzzy rules and actual causal attack mechanisms remains weak.
For example, a rule may correlate with an attack category. However, correlation does not necessarily imply causal explanation. Attackers may deliberately manipulate features to trigger misleading rules.
Thus, explanation reliability under adversarial conditions requires further investigation.

Since the paper focuses on structural uncertainty in graph anomaly detection, structural entropy provides a complementary theoretical perspective by quantifying graph complexity, hierarchy, and structural information. The paper listed below may offer potential solutions to the aforementioned issues.
Zeng X, Peng H, Li A. Proactive Bot Detection Based on Structural Information Principles[J]. IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence, 2025.
